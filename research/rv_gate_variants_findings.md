# Realized-Volatility (RV) Gate Alternatives Findings\n\nThis report evaluates three variants of the Realized Volatility (RV) gate in the **BULL-SPY** strategy sleeve, both as a standalone sleeve and within the production **60/20/20 CPM-BULL-NDX** blend.\n\n## Definitions\n- **V0 Binary (baseline):** If canary and trend and (rv_20d < rv_252d) -> SPY 100%, else best_safe 100%. (Current production)\n- **V1 Continuous vol-target:** If canary and trend -> SPY weight = min(1.0, rv_252d / rv_20d), remainder to best_safe; else best_safe 100%. (No leverage)\n- **V2 50% overlay:** If canary and trend -> if rv_20d < rv_252d SPY 100%; if rv_20d >= rv_252d SPY 50% + best_safe 50%; else best_safe 100%.\n\n### Cascade Rule (NDX Sleeve Gating)\nThe NDX sleeve gates on BULL's binary `canary_ok` AND `trend_ok` state (**NOT** the vol sizing) in all variants. This preserves the NDX circuit breaker identically across V0/V1/V2.\n\n## Clean (2008-05-30 to 2026-05-22) Performance Summary\n\n### 60/20/20 CPM-BULL-NDX Blend Metrics\n| Variant | Raw Sharpe | or V2 beat V0?**\n   - **No.** Across both the Clean and Stress windows, the baseline binary gate **V0-Prod** remains superior to both **V1 (Continuous)** and **V2 (50% Overlay)** on **Excess Sharpe** and **Calmar ratio** after turnover costs.\n   - Specifically, in the **Clean Window**, V0-Prod achieves an Excess Sharpe of **1.387** and a Calmar of **1.543**, compared to V1 (**1.358** Excess Sharpe, **1.337** Calmar) and V2 (**1.370** Excess Sharpe, **1.404** Calmar).\n   - In the **Stress Window**, V0-Prod achieves an Excess Sharpe of **1.187** and a Calmar of **1.224**, compared to V1 (**1.155** Excess Sharpe, **1.118** Calmar) and V2 (**1.163** Excess Sharpe, **1.122** Calmar).\n   - Therefore, the simple 1-bit binary RV gate is near-optimal and outperforms continuous or partial risk-scaling.\n\n2. **Turnover & Cost Savings (The Counter-Intuitive Revelation):**\n   - The original hypothesis assumed that continuous vol-targeting (V1) would trade more and incur extra transaction costs. **The actual backtest completely disproves this.**\n   - Annualized BULL sleeve turnover is **materially lower** under V1 (**431.9%**) compared to the baseline V0-Prod (**756.5%**).\n   - **Reasoning:** V0's binary gate triggers violent 100% full-portfolio flips (which cost 200% turnover each time). In contrast, V1 adjusts the SPY weight continuously and fractionally (e.g., from 90% to 85%), which results in significantly smaller net-weight modifications and less total trading volume over time.\n\n3. **Drawdown Deepening Risks:**\n   - Holding partial exposure (50% or continuous) during high-vol market drops leads to **material drawdown deepening** in the BULL sleeve and propagates directly to the 60/20/20 blend.\n   - In the Clean Window, standalone BULL sleeve MaxDD deepens from **-12.02%** (V0) to **-16.71%** (V1) and **-13.22%** (V2). This propagates to the blend, deepening blend MaxDD from **-11.62%** (V0-Prod) to **-14.07%** (V1) and **-13.37%** (V2).\n   - Thus, holding SPY exposure through high-vol drop regimes (even at 50% size) is highly damaging and more than offsets the benefit of faster re-entry.\n\n4. **V-Rebound Lag Resolution:**\n   - Month-by-month tracking confirms that V0 suffers from severe re-entry lag. For instance, in February 2019 and May 2020, V0 held **0% SPY**, completely missing the initial recovery surge. In contrast, V1 held **94.4%** and **85.2%** SPY respectively, and V2 held **50.0%** SPY, capturing the initial rebound.\n   - While this faster re-entry improves the standalone BULL CAGR (12.78% for V1 and 12.57% for V2 vs 11.77% for V0), the accompanying volatility and drawdown deepening during down markets ruin the risk-adjusted returns.\n\n## Conclusion\nThe binary RV gate (`v_20d < v_252d`) is **highly robust and superior** to continuous or 50% overlay variants. Although V1 and V2 successfully resolve the V-rebound lag and significantly reduce annualized turnover, they holding SPY during high-vol drops creates deeper drawdowns that erode both Sharpe and Calmar ratios. **The current production binary V0 gate remains the optimal and recommended choice.**
